## 1. Understanding Your Rate Calendar

The Rate Calendar is your command center: every room, every night, individually priced.

- **Demand score (0–10):** how much pressure the model sees for that date. 7+ means demand
  is strong; you can hold or raise. Below 4 means soft; consider a strategic discount.
- **Recommendation colors:**
  - **Green (premium)** — recommended above your rack midpoint.
  - **Gray (at rack)** — holding at your normal rate.
  - **Rose (discount)** — a strategic reduction to fill a night that would otherwise go empty.
- **Status dots / weekend tint:** Friday and Saturday cells are shaded; a 🎉 marks an active
  demand event.
- **Minimum-stay badges:** appear when the engine recommends a 2- or 3-night minimum (typically
  around festivals and strong weekends) to protect weekend revenue.
- **Gap-night tags:** flag an "orphan" night stranded between two booked dates — see Revenue
  Intelligence for fill actions.
- **Water Festival gold:** the biggest Beaufort demand window is highlighted in gold; the engine
  starts pricing it up ~90 days out.

**Reading it daily:** scan for green (capture upside), rose (act to fill), and any 🎉/min-stay
badges. Click any cell for the full reasoning.

## 2. Reading Demand Forecasts

Each recommendation blends several signals:
- **Seasonal index** — your market's typical strength for that month.
- **Event multiplier** — festivals, graduations, and local events.
- **Lead time** — how close the date is and how fast it's filling.
- **Competitor average** — what comparable rooms are going for.
- **Reputation premium** — the cushion your reviews justify.

**Interpreting the demand score:** treat it as confidence in *upward* pressure. High score +
HIGH confidence = trust it and hold/raise. **When to override:** you have private knowledge the
model can't see — a wedding block, a road closure, a big local event not yet in the calendar.
Override freely; the model learns from the pattern over time.

## 3. Competitive Intelligence

- **Price ladder:** your rate shown against the comp set across the date range, so you can see
  exactly where you sit.
- **Tier badges:** Direct Competitors (highest weight), Market Reference, and Luxury/Budget
  **Reference** anchors (shown but excluded from the comp-set average).
- **Like-for-like:** filter by room category (Waterfront, Garden, Suites…). Properties without a
  comparable room type gray out and show **N/A** — you're never compared apples-to-oranges.
- **Rate-drop alerts:** when a competitor cuts rates overnight, you get an alert. A single drop is
  often nerves — hold firm unless your *strongest* direct competitor moves.
- **Sold-out signals:** when comps sell out, that's room for you to raise.
- **Add/remove competitors:** Settings → Competitors, or during onboarding.

## 4. Autopilot — Set It and Trust It

- **Guardrails:** your **min** (floor) and **max** (ceiling) rates per room. Autopilot can move
  *within* these only — it can never underprice or overprice beyond your limits.
- **Confidence setting:** Conservative / Balanced / Aggressive controls how large and how often
  Autopilot adjusts.
- **What triggers an auto-publish:** a recommendation that clears the confidence threshold for that
  room and stays inside your guardrails.
- **Weekly performance report:** every week you get a summary of what Autopilot changed and the
  estimated revenue impact.
- **When to adjust:** if you find yourself overriding the same direction repeatedly, nudge the
  confidence setting or your base/min/max to match your instinct.

## 5. Guest CRM — Building Your Guest Database

- **WiFi capture setup:** connect your guest WiFi splash page so returning guests are recognized
  (optional but powerful for win-back).
- **Email consent:** only consented guests enter campaigns — INNtelligence keeps you compliant.
- **Segments explained:** VIP (multi-stay, high spend), Regular, New, and **Lapsed** (180+ days
  since last stay). Lapsed VIPs are your highest-ROI outreach.
- **Demand-triggered campaigns:** when a soft period or gap appears, INNtelligence drafts a
  targeted email to the right segment (e.g., past waterfront guests within 150 miles).
- **Approving campaigns:** review the draft, edit if you like, and send. Nothing goes out without
  your approval.

## 6. Demand Alerts — What They Mean and What To Do

- **Surge alert:** demand spiking — **raise rates now**, before the date fills at lower prices.
- **Competitor drop:** a comp cut rates — **hold firm** if your strongest competitor hasn't moved;
  you'll be alerted if they do.
- **Low occupancy:** a soft window ahead — **consider a campaign** to past guests, or a modest,
  strategic discount.
- **Festival / event:** a demand window is approaching — **check your minimum-stay settings** so a
  one-night booking doesn't block a lucrative multi-night stay.

## 7. Reading Your Performance Reports

- **RevPAR (Revenue per Available Room):** room revenue ÷ total rooms. The single best measure of
  pricing health — it rewards both rate and occupancy.
- **ADR (Average Daily Rate):** average rate of *sold* rooms. Rising ADR with steady occupancy is
  the goal.
- **Comparing to last year:** every metric shows a year-over-year delta so you can see real
  progress, not just month-to-month noise.
- **Autopilot lift:** the documented additional revenue from dynamic pricing versus a flat-rate
  strategy. On a typical boutique inn, a conservative 15% revenue lift (~$1,244/month) against a
  $699 Professional subscription is about a **1.8× return** — and it climbs past 2× at 20%.

## 8. FAQs

**How often do recommendations update?** Every morning, after the overnight competitor scrape
(typically by 6 AM local).

**Can I override a recommendation?** Always. Click the cell → Override → enter your rate. Your
overrides help the model learn your property.

**What if I disagree with a recommendation?** Override it. If you disagree often in the same
direction, adjust your base/min/max or Autopilot confidence so the model matches your strategy.

**Data privacy.** Your booking and guest data is yours. We use it to price *your* property and to
build anonymized market benchmarks — never to share your specifics with another inn.
